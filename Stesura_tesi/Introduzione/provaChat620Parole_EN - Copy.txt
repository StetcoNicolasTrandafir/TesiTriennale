In the digital age, recommendation systems are essential tools for enhancing user experiences, particularly in the music domain. From streaming platforms to personalized playlists, these systems aim to provide users with tailored suggestions that align with their preferences and habits. However, many existing solutions rely on "black box" algorithms, such as deep neural networks, which lack transparency in their decision-making processes. This lack of explainability often leaves users in the dark about why a particular recommendation was made, eroding trust and acceptance. Additionally, these systems are often prone to the so-called filter bubble effect: a feedback loop where users with similar preferences are repeatedly exposed to the same content. This not only limits diversity but also reduces the relevance of suggestions in addressing individual needs or exploring new possibilities.

This project introduces a music recommendation system designed to address these challenges by combining ontological logic with explainable artificial intelligence (XAI). The proposed system eliminates the opacity typical of black-box algorithms and avoids the formation of homogenous clusters. By grounding its recommendations in the user's emotional state and their desired emotional direction, the system offers personalized and meaningful suggestions that break free from the limitations of traditional approaches. Additionally, it provides users with clear explanations for each recommendation, fostering trust and engagement.

The core innovation lies in the integration of emotion-driven logic. Recognizing that music often serves as a reflection of, or a tool to influence, emotional states, the system begins by identifying the user’s current mood and their emotional goal. This is achieved through a series of four intuitive visual steps. First, the user selects an emoticon corresponding to one of Plutchik’s eight primary emotions, providing a quick and clear initial indication of their mood. Next, the selection is refined by choosing a nuanced color that represents the specific shade of the emotion—whether it is dark and intense or light and mellow. This allows the system to capture the complexity of human emotions beyond simple categorizations.

In the third step, the user is presented with situational images designed to represent facets of the eight primary emotions, such as a sunny field of flowers for joy or a lone figure on a rainy park bench for sadness. Users are asked to identify which images resonate most and least with their current emotional state, providing additional insights into their comfort zones and preferences. These responses are then validated through a final round of selections to confirm the direction the user wishes to take: whether to maintain their current mood or shift towards a more desired state.

Once the emotional data is collected, the system employs an ontological framework to process the information. Attributes associated with each emotion—both rigid and probabilistic—are carefully mapped to musical characteristics. This mapping is informed by empirical survey data and structured to ensure consistency and accuracy. At this stage, the system leverages a recommendation engine inspired by the "NervousGPT" project, which utilized ontological logic to suggest songs based on the attributes of two user-defined musical genres. In this implementation, the emotional attributes are directly translated into musical attributes, creating a seamless connection between the user's emotional needs and the music they are recommended.

This approach not only ensures precise and relevant suggestions but also provides a transparent rationale for each recommendation. For instance, if the system suggests an uplifting track to help a user transition from sadness to a more positive state, it can explicitly outline how the track’s attributes—such as tempo, harmony, and lyrical themes—align with the user’s emotional goals. This level of explainability sets the system apart from traditional black-box models, making it both more trustworthy and user-friendly.

Furthermore, by avoiding the pitfalls of the filter bubble effect, the system encourages diversity and exploration. Instead of confining users to a repetitive cycle of similar suggestions, it dynamically adapts to their emotional context, offering fresh and meaningful content that resonates with their current state of mind. This adaptability enhances the overall user experience and redefines how music can be used as a tool for emotional regulation and expression.

In conclusion, the proposed system represents a significant step forward in the evolution of music recommendation technology. By combining emotional intelligence, ontological logic, and explainability, it addresses key shortcomings of existing systems while opening new possibilities for personalized and meaningful interactions. This innovative approach not only enhances the way users discover and engage with music but also demonstrates the potential of AI to bridge the gap between technology and human emotions in a transparent and impactful manner.
